● Sales ● Business Development ● Operations ● Finance ● Management
Role structure: Sales owns the opportunity from a commercial standpoint — creating and managing it. Business Development is the coordinating role that follows the opportunity through Operations, Finance, and Management until every stage is complete. Operations is the single department responsible for the site visit, budget preparation, project creation, task assignment, execution, and closure — there is no separate project team.

4 Key Decision Points

The points where the process branches based on a business condition or human decision.

Is a Site Visit required?

Determined by Operations based on the nature of the project, before the Budget Builder begins.

Does the Project Manager approve the Budget?

The first approval gate after Operations prepares the budget; rejection sends it back for revision within Operations.

Does Finance approve the Budget?

Finance reviews only the Financial Summary (cost, margin, allowance).

Does Management approve the Budget?

The final, decisive approval; only after this does the Budget move to "Approved" status.

Does the customer approve the Quotation?

A revision request sends Sales back to update the quotation; approval moves directly to Sales Order creation.

7 Department User Journeys

What each department does, step by step, from its own perspective.

🔵 Sales

  1. Creates the Opportunity with customer & project details
  2. Tracks progress via the Opportunity Dashboard
  3. Creates the Quotation once the budget is approved
  4. Adds the commercial margin and sends the quotation to the customer
  5. Follows up on customer approval and creates the Sales Order

🔷 Business Development

  1. Receives the Opportunity from Sales as soon as it's qualified
  2. Coordinates its handover to Operations
  3. Follows the budget through all three approval stages
  4. Ensures the opportunity never stalls at any stage without follow-up

🟠 Operations

  1. Receives the assigned Opportunity
  2. Determines and, if needed, performs the Site Visit
  3. Prepares the full Budget and secures Project Manager approval internally
  4. Creates the Project from a Template once the Sales Order exists
  5. Assigns tasks and executes the project through to closure

🟢 Finance

  1. Receives the Budget after Project Manager approval
  2. Reviews only the Financial Summary
  3. Approves or rejects the budget, stating a clear reason

🟣 Management

  1. Receives the Budget after Finance approval
  2. Conducts the final, comprehensive review
  3. Gives final approval or rejection with remarks
  4. Tracks project performance from high-level dashboards

11 Suggestions to Improve Operational Performance

Business-level considerations for discussion with the departments.

1

Automatic approval thresholds by budget value

Small budgets may not require Management approval, relying instead on the Project Manager and Finance only.

2

Set an SLA for each approval stage

For example: 24 hours for the Project Manager, 48 hours for Finance, 48 hours for Management, with automatic alerts when a deadline is exceeded.

3

Require a documented reason for every rejection

This avoids repeated revision cycles without a clear understanding of what's actually needed.

4

A single clear owner for the "Is a Site Visit required?" decision

Designating the Operations Manager as the sole decision owner avoids inconsistent, ad-hoc judgment calls.

5

Standardized project templates, reviewed periodically

Periodic review of project templates ensures they reflect the actual types of projects being delivered.

6

A cycle-time KPI for the full process

Measuring the time from opportunity creation to project closure as a comprehensive key performance indicator.

7

Track customer response time on the quotation

Measuring the time between sending the quotation and receiving customer approval helps identify opportunities to speed up the commercial stage.

8

One documented handover point between Business Development and Operations

A clear handover checklist when the opportunity moves from Business Development to Operations ensures all required data is complete.
